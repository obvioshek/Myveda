02 — Why we are building this

Social media got loud. It does not have to stay that way.

Most feeds are designed to keep you scrolling, and what spreads fastest is often what makes people angriest. The good parts of being online — sharing, learning, meeting people — deserve a better place.

  • Rewards time spentNoise

    Endless feeds, autoplay, and alerts compete for every spare minute.

  • Rewards numbersPerformance

    Likes and follower counts turn sharing into keeping score.

  • Rewards reactionsOutrage

    The sharpest reply travels furthest, so conversations turn into contests.

A better way to be online.

  1. Express thoughtfully

    Post what you mean, with a little context, so people know what they are reading.

  2. Engage meaningfully

    Reply to understand, ask a follow-up, and disagree without making it personal.

  3. Share responsibly

    Pass things on with your own take, and keep the original context attached.

Reply the way you normally would. If a thread heats up and you post several replies in a row, you get a short pause before the next one. Nothing is deleted.

Why a pause, not a block

A hard stop can feel like punishment. A brief pause creates room to reconsider without taking the conversation away. Same moment of friction, a different invitation.

Try it: post three replies quickly. The pause here is shortened to fifteen seconds so you can watch it finish.

05 — From the house of Veda Verse

Old ideas, read for the way we live now.

Short, sourced reads from our editors on Indian culture — where a practice came from, what it asked of people, and what is still worth carrying today.

TraditionEveryday life · 6 min read

Why rangoli was drawn with rice flour

A doorway drawing that was meant to disappear.

In many homes — especially in the south, where it is called kolam — the morning drawing at the threshold was made with rice flour, and in some homes wheat flour. It was never meant to last. By midday, ants had carried off the fine lines, and sparrows and squirrels had picked at the rest.

Documented

Flour designs at the doorway are drawn across much of India, and ants, birds, and squirrels do eat them.

Told

That the drawing began as a daily meal left for small creatures. Many families explain it this way; the practice is older than the records that could confirm it.

Worth carryingBegin the day by leaving something for someone who can never thank you.

ContextHistory · 5 min read

Why did India's freedom arrive at midnight?

And which country, freed by the same law, did not wait for the clock?

The date came from a law and a viceroy. The hour, many people say, came from the stars. Both halves of the story are worth knowing — and worth keeping apart.

Documented

The Indian Independence Act 1947 set 15 August as the day two new dominions, India and Pakistan, came into being. Mountbatten had chosen the date: the second anniversary of Japan's surrender.

Told

That astrologers found the 15th inauspicious, and midnight — a moment belonging to neither day — became the compromise for the handover in Delhi. The story is widely retold; how far it decided the hour is still debated.

Show the answer

Pakistan. Its handover ceremony was held in Karachi on 14 August, and it celebrates its independence on that day.

Sources: Indian Independence Act 1947 · published accounts of the transfer of power

Worth carryingTell the good story — and label it. Keep what is recorded and what is remembered side by side, not blended.

06 — Principles

Principles you can see in the product.

Good intentions are easy to write down. These are the commitments behind the design, and the places you would notice them.

In plain words

Seven principles we build by.

Agency

You choose what reaches you and when. No autoplay, no infinite scroll, and no ranking built around keeping you scrolling.

Context

Posts and replies carry context. Sources can stay attached, and sharing adds your perspective instead of stripping the original.

Plurality

No single viewpoint is treated as the default. People can contribute different experiences and perspectives, and useful ideas can travel across disagreement.

Contribution

Recognition comes from what you add to a conversation and the people you help—not from likes or follower totals.

Calm

Messages can arrive in windows you choose. Heated conversations can slow down without turning every pause into a penalty.

Privacy

Collect as little as practical. No ads and no data sales. When you ask for deletion, we tell you what can be removed and what a legal requirement prevents.

Community responsibility

Community standards are applied with accountable human review. Serious legal requests have a separate process and a transparency record.

  1. 01

    Question ideas, respect people

    People bring different backgrounds, values, and experiences. Challenge an idea without turning the person behind it into the target.

  2. 02

    No status scoring. Not anywhere.

    No hidden social score, class, or popularity tier shapes what people can see or how they are treated.

  3. 03

    The product should stay accountable to its purpose

    The long-term model should protect the product from quietly becoming an attention business. The goal is to be funded by people who want the platform to exist, not by selling their attention or data.

And here is what could prove us wrong.

Good intentions are not evidence. Here are three product claims we would test—and the results that would make us change the design.

  1. The claim

    A feed with a clear end can feel better than a feed with no end.

    What would change it

    If sustained use falls because the feed ends, we revisit the design rather than pretending the measure is wrong.

  2. The claim

    Restating someone's view before replying improves understanding.

    What would change it

    If independent reviewers cannot distinguish the restatements from a control, we remove the gate.

  3. The claim

    Predictable message windows can reduce distraction without reducing contribution.

    What would change it

    If contribution drops materially, we change the delivery model rather than defending the friction.

When the product is tested, participants should consent, know the purpose, and receive the results. Findings should be published clearly, including outcomes that go against the design.

Questions people ask.

When can I sign in?

Invitations go out in stages, starting with the early list. You will get one email when yours is ready, and nothing before then.

Do I need to know anything about Indian culture?

No. Most conversations are about everyday life — food, books, work, cities, family, and ideas. Indian life and traditions come up naturally, because they are part of many members' lives. If you are curious, you are welcome.

Is this a religious app?

No. It is a social platform. People talk about everyday life, culture, science, work, and values without being asked to adopt any worldview. Nobody is asked to believe anything, and nobody is mocked for what they believe.

No ads and no data sales. So how is it paid for?

Joining and taking part stay free. The platform is meant to be funded by members and patrons who choose to support it, with open finances. If that means staying smaller, we would rather stay smaller than become an attention business.
